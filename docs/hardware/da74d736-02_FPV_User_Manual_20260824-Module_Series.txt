Precautions

1.  Never power on the gimbal when it is inverted. This operation will cause motor overload and permanent damage to the gimbal.

2.  Under all circumstances (powered on or powered off), do not aim the infrared detection window of this product directly at high-intensity radiation sources including the sun, carbon dioxide lasers and welding machines. Otherwise, the camera will be burned, resulting in irreversible damage.

3.  Do not place the camera in locations with direct sunlight and poor ventilation, or near heat sources such as radiators and heaters.

4.  Avoid frequent startup and shutdown of the device. Wait at least 30 seconds after power-off before restarting; otherwise, the service life of the camera core will be shortened.

5.  Before installing the gimbal, ensure all interfaces and surfaces are dry and free of moisture.

6.  Confirm the gimbal is firmly mounted on the UAV before use.

7.  The single continuous laser ranging duration is recommended not to exceed 15 minutes.

8.  Do not hot-swap the Micro SD card while the device is powered on during operation.

9.  Never touch the coating of the camera lens with bare hands or scratch it with hard objects, which will blur imaging and degrade image quality.

10. When cleaning the camera lens, only wipe the lens surface with a soft, dry cleaning cloth. Alkaline cleaning agents are strictly prohibited.

11. Handle the product gently during storage. Do not drop it; otherwise, the equipment may malfunction.

12. It is strictly forbidden to transport the gimbal mounted directly on the drone. The gimbal must be properly packed in a dedicated transport case for safe transit.

1 Product Overview

The CPDD075 series is a suite of visual modules and stabilization gimbals centered on AI-based tracking, offering various configurations including single-light, dual-light, and triple-light systems. Designed specifically for drone and anti-drone applications, these products deliver high-precision target recognition and tracking solutions, with all units built around the end-to-end workflow of "target perception – locking – tracking – guidance".

This product series is compatible with multiple mainstream flight control platforms in China, enabling seamless integration. It can also be connected to the RemoteControl display and control software platform for rapid deployment of drone systems.

1.1 Product Highlights

High-definition fixed-focus visible light

It provides high-quality image input, supports ultra-low-latency analog output, and synchronizes target motion status in real time to prevent tracking lag or target escape caused by low frame rates.

Infrared

The system acquires infrared thermal radiation imaging of the object itself, where higher temperatures correspond to greater radiation intensity. It is unaffected by lighting conditions, smoke, or camouflage interference, requires no external ambient light source, and can stably capture thermal signals from personnel, vehicles, and drones even in complete darkness.

AI Recognition

Developed using cutting-edge AI recognition technology, this identification model achieves exceptionally high accuracy and recognition rates for typical targets (people, vehicles, balloons, drones), while automatically adjusting the tracking aperture size to maintain precise focus on the target.

1.2 Application Scenarios

FPV UAV Visual Module

It supports drones in first-person flight operations, enabling autonomous detection and identification of targets such as individuals and vehicles within the field of view, with capabilities for target locking and continuous tracking, providing precise visual situational awareness and flight control support for drone inspection and reconnaissance missions.

Anti-drone collaborative detection

As a low-altitude maneuver detection unit, it works in conjunction with an anti-drone system to rapidly approach suspicious aircraft; its visual module precisely identifies and tracks unauthorized drones, providing high-definition close-range footage for evidence collection, while coordinating with jamming equipment to drive away or intercept the targets.

1.3 Working Principle

-   Step 1: Target Perception

The camera module adapts to environmental changes and works collaboratively to achieve comprehensive data acquisition with no blind spots.

1. During daytime or in high-visibility conditions:

The short-focus visible-light camera first performs a "wide-area scan" to rapidly capture targets such as people, vehicles, and drones across extensive scenes. Upon detecting a target, it switches to the long-focus visible-light camera to focus on detailed features (e.g., drone identification marks or personnel movements) and acquire high-definition image data.

2. Night/low-visibility scenarios (for infrared-enabled models):

The infrared camera (operating at 8–14 μm long wavelengths) activates without requiring ambient light, capturing thermal radiation from targets—including human body temperature and drone motor heat sources—to generate thermal imaging data. It maintains clear target contours even in complete darkness or fog/tight conditions, effectively preventing the "failure" of visible-light cameras.

-   Step 2: Data preprocessing – Optimize data quality to lay the foundation for AI analysis

The image processing board performs "cleaning and optimization" on the raw data, removing noise and standardizing formats to ensure subsequent AI algorithms can accurately identify them.

1. Single-camera data optimization:

Visible light data: performs white balance correction (to restore true colors) and noise reduction (to eliminate image noise caused by drone shake).

Infrared data: Perform non-uniformity correction (eliminate dark current interference in the infrared detector and prevent uneven image brightness).

2. Multi-camera data fusion (Dual/Triple camera models):

Using a pixel-level fusion algorithm, infrared thermal imaging (showcasing the target's prominent outline) is overlaid with visible-light imagery (capturing environmental details) to generate a composite image combining thermal features and scene background (e.g., "identifying vehicles in infrared while showing their location on the road in visible light"). Additionally, short-focus "wide-area coordinates" are calibrated against long-focus "detail coordinates" to ensure consistent target positioning.

3. Format Conversion

Convert the optimized data into a standardized format recognizable by AI algorithms (e.g., RGB or grayscale), while simultaneously compressing video data using H.264/H.265 encoding to reduce subsequent bandwidth requirements during transmission.

-   Step 3: AI Target Processing – Core Decision-Making to Achieve Autonomous Tracking

This constitutes the core of the product's "intelligent" functionality, where a tracker (integrated into the servo controller) loads a pre-trained AI model and makes three critical decisions with support from an image processing board:

1. Target Detection and Recognition:

The AI model performs frame-level scanning on preprocessed images, automatically identifying object types (human, vehicle, or drone) based on feature matching (e.g., human contours, vehicle shapes, drone dimensions), eliminating background interference (such as trees or buildings), and annotating the pixel coordinates of targets in the image.

2. Tracking, Prediction, and Locking:

Using the Kalman filtering algorithm, the system predicts the target's position in the next frame based on its historical motion trajectory (e.g., drone flight direction or pedestrian walking speed). Even when temporarily obstructed (e.g., by passing trees), it retains the trajectory information to prevent tracking interruptions and generates locking commands to ensure the camera/gimbal remains consistently aligned with the target.

3. Target Positioning (Space Coordinate Calculation):

The image processing board integrates camera parameters and the drone's own position (provided by an external flight controller) to convert the target's pixel coordinates into precise spatial coordinates (e.g., latitude, longitude, distance), achieving a unified "CEP 10-meter" positioning accuracy across the product series and delivering target location data to external devices such as anti-drone systems.

-   Step 4: Execution Control – Hardware Integration for Stable Tracking

The servo controller receives AI-driven commands to control camera/gimbal movements while communicating with external devices (drone flight controllers and ground stations), enabling both tracking execution and data output.

1. Camera Angle Control:

The servo controller uses AI-predicted target positions to drive the camera to fine-tune its angle (e.g., when the target moves to the right, the camera rotates to the right), ensuring that the target remains consistently at the center of the frame.

2. Gimbal Image Stabilization Control:

Jitter Detection: The image processing board utilizes "image feature point matching" to analyze the offset between adjacent frames (e.g., image displacement caused by drone roll or pitch jitter);

3. Compensation Command: Send a reverse compensation command to the gimbal (e.g., when the drone sways right, the gimbal rotates left). The dual-axis mechanical gyroscope responds rapidly, achieving an image stabilization accuracy of ≤0.02° and compensating for the impact of shake on tracking performance.

4. Angle range control: The pan-tilt head rotates within preset ranges (roll: -70° to +70°; pitch: -140° to +95°) to avoid exceeding mechanical limits.

5. Data Output:

Video Output: Send the compressed tracking video (including target markers) to the ground station or drone video transmission system via the "Network/Analog Video Interface".

6. Control signal output: Through the "TTL serial port/SBUS interface", send the target position and tracking status (e.g., "locked/lost") to the drone's flight controller or anti-drone system, assisting external devices in decision-making (e.g., initiating anti-drone interference).

-   Step 5: State Assurance – Environmental Adaptation + Exception Handling to Ensure Continuous Operation

The product adapts to complex environments and handles anomalies through "real-time monitoring + dynamic adjustment," ensuring uninterrupted workflow:

Environmental Adaptation Adjustment:

1. Temperature adaptation: The sensor continuously monitors operating temperature (-20°C to +55°C). When temperatures are too low, the image processing board automatically reduces non-critical computations (e.g., simplifying image fusion algorithms) to prevent hardware lag.

2. Rain Protection: The housing design combined with waterproofing of internal circuits ensures that the camera and interfaces remain dry during light rain conditions, maintaining normal data acquisition and transmission.

exception handling:

1. Target loss: If the AI tracker fails to detect the target (e.g., when it exits the field of view), it automatically triggers a "short-focus wide-area scan" to re-search for the target;

2. Hardware failure: The servo controller monitors the camera's power supply (DC 10.8 V–26.4 V) and interface communication status. If the camera disconnects or voltage abnormalities occur, it immediately sends an alarm signal to the ground station and switches to "Manual Control Mode" to prevent complete failure.

2 Installation Method

1. The CPDD075 is installed in an integrated configuration;

2. For integrated installation, mount the camera and control circuit board separately: install the camera using the mechanical mounting interface on the rear top surface, while mounting the control board directly onto the customer's product. Ensure the distance between the circuit board interface and the camera remains within 50 mm.

3. When installing the device, heat dissipation of the main circuit board must be taken into full consideration. The main heat-generating area is the shielding cover. If adequate heat dissipation measures cannot be guaranteed, a cooling fan can be installed as an optional accessory.

2.1 Connection Between the gimbal and the UAV

2.1.1 Power Supply

The gimbal's power interface uses GH1.25mm terminals, with one end connected to the gimbal's power port and the other to the drone's power output port. For optimal performance, use a DC voltage of 10.8 V–26.4 V at 3 A as specified for GH1.25mm terminals.

2.1.2 Network

The gimbal's external network interface uses an RJ45 connector, allowing direct connection to a video transmission port for viewing or controlling the gimbal via network video streaming.

2.1.3 SBUS/CRSF Interface

The gimbal supports remote control via SBUS, CRSF, or the transition from SBUS to CRSF protocols. Channel configuration is detailed in Section 4.

2.1.4 Control Serial Port

3 Gimbal Control

3.1 Network Control

Connect the gimbal's network port to the data link's sky-side port, and connect the PC to the data link's ground-side port. After configuring the PC's network segment to match that of the gimbal, launch the RemoteControl software on the PC to control the gimbal via the network.

3.2 Serial Port Control

Switch the gimbal to the required serial port mode and connect the corresponding cable. Connect the gimbal's serial port to the data link's airborne unit, while connecting the PC to its ground unit. On the PC, launch the RemoteControl software, configure the correct COM ports and baud rate, and you can then control the gimbal via the serial interface.

3.3 SBUS/CRSF Control

Connect the gimbal's SBUS/CRSF interface to the receiver, then map the remote control joysticks to the corresponding channels according to the gimbal's operational requirements to operate it remotely.

3.4 Network Video Output

Connect the gimbal's network port to the data link's sky-side port, and connect the PC to the data link's ground-side port. After configuring the PC's network segment to match that of the gimbal, open the RemoteControl display control software or another streaming client on the PC and enter the streaming address to access the gimbal's network video feed.
